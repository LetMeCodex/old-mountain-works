# 06 — SUSPENSION MODEL

Current suspension should be upgraded from a basic constraint-only feel into a more deliberate spring-damper model.

Use:

$$
F_s = -k x - c v
$$

where:

```text
k = suspension stiffness
c = damping coefficient
x = suspension displacement
v = suspension displacement velocity
```

Clamp the suspension travel:

$$
x_{clamped} = clamp(x,-x_{max},x_{max})
$$

Add bottom-out handling when:

$$
|x| \ge x_{max}
$$

At bottom-out:

* increase impact force
* generate stronger camera response
* generate mechanical audio
* generate particles
* increase rollover risk only when physically justified

Do not instantly kill the player.

# 07 — TIRE CONTACT MODEL

For each wheel determine:

```text
contact
surface normal
surface tangent
penetration
relative velocity
wheel angular velocity
slip
```

Calculate tangent vector:

$$
T = normalize(1,-n_x/n_y)
$$

or construct a proper perpendicular basis from the surface normal.

Calculate wheel contact velocity:

$$
v_{contact}=v_{wheel}+\omega \times r
$$

Then determine longitudinal slip approximately as:

$$
\kappa =
\frac{v_{wheel}-v_{ground}}
{\max(|v_{ground}|,\epsilon)}
$$

Clamp the value to avoid numerical instability.

---

# 08 — TRACTION MODEL

Do not simply use:

```javascript
wheel.friction = 0.92;
```

as the entire driving model.

Create a controllable traction response.

A simplified tire force model can be:

$$
F_t = \mu N \cdot S(\kappa)
$$

where:

```text
μ = surface friction coefficient
N = normal force
S = slip response
```

Use a saturating function such as:

$$
S(\kappa)=\frac{\kappa}{|\kappa|+\kappa_0}
$$

This prevents absurd forces at extreme slip.

Tune it visually and empirically.

---

# 09 — NORMAL FORCE

Approximate wheel load from gravity + chassis dynamics.

For a simple static distribution:

$$
N_{rear} + N_{front} \approx mg
$$

Then modify dynamically based on:

* acceleration
* braking
* slope
* pitch
* suspension compression

This should influence:

* traction
* wheelspin
* braking
* wheelie tendency
* landing response

# 10 — ENGINE FORCE

Instead of constant torque:

```text
engineTorque = constant
```

introduce an engine response curve.

Conceptually:

$$
\tau_{engine} =
\tau_{max} \cdot C(\omega)
$$

where:

```text
ω = wheel / engine rotational speed
C = torque curve
```

Create a normalized torque curve:

```text
0 RPM
↓
strong low-end
↓
peak torque
↓
falloff
↓
redline
```

This makes throttle more controllable.

---

# 11 — DRIVE FORCE

Convert wheel torque into longitudinal force:

$$
F = \frac{\tau}{r}
$$

where:

```text
τ = applied wheel torque
r = wheel radius
```

Then cap force based on available traction:

$$
F_{usable} = min(F_{engine},\mu N)
$$

This is critical.

The car should not magically generate unlimited horizontal acceleration on a steep mountain.

---

# 12 — BRAKING

Brake torque:

$$
\tau_b = \tau_{max} \cdot input
$$

But braking force must also respect available traction.

Avoid instant velocity cancellation.

The player should be able to lock/slip tires on low-grip surfaces.

# 13 — CENTER OF MASS

Expose:

```javascript
vehicle.centerOfMass
vehicle.mass
vehicle.inertia
vehicle.frontWeightBias
```

For a two-wheelbase vehicle:

$$
N_f = mg\frac{L-x_{COM}}{L}
$$

$$
N_r = mg\frac{x_{COM}}{L}
$$

where:

```text
L = wheelbase
xCOM = COM distance from rear contact
```

Use this to calculate approximate dynamic weight distribution.

# 14 — ROLLOVER PHYSICS

The vehicle should not flip randomly.

Create a rollover model based on:

* center of mass height
* wheelbase
* lateral/normal acceleration
* terrain angle
* angular velocity
* contact configuration

A basic static tipping condition can be approximated from:

$$
a_{critical} \approx g\frac{b}{h}
$$

where:

```text
b = half-width / effective support distance
h = COM height
```

For longitudinal rollover, use the equivalent wheelbase/contact geometry.

This is not a replacement for Matter.js.

It is a **diagnostic / assist / prediction model**.

Use it to understand when the vehicle is approaching instability.

Use:

\tau_{assist}

The purpose is to remove numerical jitter, NOT remove skill.

# 16 — TERRAIN NORMAL

For terrain height function:

$$
y=f(x)
$$

the local slope is:

$$
\frac{dy}{dx}=f'(x)
$$

and tangent:

$$
T =
\frac{(1,f'(x))}
{\sqrt{1+(f'(x))^2}}
$$

The normal becomes:

$$
N =
\frac{(-f'(x),1)}
{\sqrt{1+(f'(x))^2}}
$$

Use the normal for:

* wheel contact
* terrain orientation
* landing quality
* visual alignment
* slope classification
* traction calculations
* debug display

# 65 — INTERPOLATION

Because physics runs at a fixed timestep, interpolate visuals:

$$
P_{render}
=
P_{previous}
+
\alpha(P_{current}-P_{previous})
$$

where:

$$
\alpha =
\frac{accumulator}{fixedDelta}
$$

This reduces visual jitter without modifying physics.
